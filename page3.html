Mapping Callery Pear Tree Density in New York City
Kernel Density Estimation (Raster) · Species Filtering (Attribute Query) · Zonal Statistics (Mean by NTA) · Jenks Classification (Choropleth) · CSV → Point Conversion

This project maps the spatial distribution of Callery pear trees—an invasive species—across New York City to identify areas of highest concentration and inform targeted removal strategies. Using the 2015 NYC Street Tree Census and 2020 Neighborhood Tabulation Areas (NTAs), the analysis filters 58,931 Callery pear records from the full dataset and converts them into a point layer for spatial analysis. In QGIS, a kernel density estimation is applied using a 2,640-foot radius and 100-foot cell size to generate a continuous raster surface representing tree concentration. Zonal statistics are then used to calculate mean density values for each NTA, and results are classified using Jenks natural breaks to identify priority areas. The findings show that Callery pear trees are not evenly distributed across the city but instead form two major hotspots. The most significant concentration occurs in southwestern Staten Island, while a secondary cluster appears along Manhattan’s east side, particularly the Upper East Side and Yorkville. The NTA ranking (Table 1, page 5) confirms that the highest-density areas are concentrated in Staten Island, with Manhattan containing a broader set of high-density neighborhoods. By combining raster density modeling with neighborhood-level aggregation, the project demonstrates how GIS can identify localized environmental risks and support targeted ecological management and replanting efforts.
